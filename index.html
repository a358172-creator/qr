THE KNOWLEDGE BEHIND THE MODEL

Notes & references

Four conceptual tours: glutamatergic excitotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, microglial remodeling and structural spine plasticity. Proportions, concentrations and dynamics are simplified; mechanisms can coexist and reinforce one another. Injury is not an inevitable outcome.

The cutaway reveals vesicles, receptors and cristae; it does not represent a rupture. In B, the mitochondrion occupies the proximal dendrite connected to the spine. Reuptake, astrocytes, Mg²⁺ and coagonists are omitted. Colors identify structures; they are not natural colors.

The second tour interprets panel D and the third panel C of the reference scientific figure. Microglia approach an altered spine while another remains preserved. The fourth tour interprets panel A: postsynaptic organization, actin and physiological remodeling. The general apoptosis module remains unavailable. The experience does not estimate individual susceptibility.

In B, local caspase-3 and spine retraction are distinguished from the alternative cytochrome c–Apaf-1–caspase-9–executioner pathway. The latter can contribute to apoptosis; it is not obligatory after remodeling and does not demonstrate complete neuronal death. Cytochrome c moves from the intermembrane space into the cytosol in the context of outer membrane permeabilization.

NO and O₂•⁻ can react to form ONOO⁻. PTP indicates inner membrane permeability transition, distinct from outer membrane permeabilization. AIF can participate in caspase-independent injury; it does not pass through this pore. Its nuclear destination is spatially compressed without placing a nucleus inside the dendrite.

The original anatomical Figure 1 is not accessible in this revision. The scope described in the documentation is retained and compared with the sources below. The mechanism atlas does not present experimental measurements. The Experimental evidence section separately displays the supplied figures from the homogenate study.

In panel C, C1q and C3 are represented as conceptual components of synaptic recognition, not universal markers of injury. Caspase-3 indicates local signaling within the spine: neither an apoptotic cascade nor an extracellular attraction signal is shown. Microglial contact and remodeling are context-dependent possibilities.

In panel A, PSD-95, DISC1 and Kalirin-7 occupy conceptual positions related to spine organization. The tour guides attention without proposing an obligatory molecular chain. Moderate Ca²⁺ entry and shape changes illustrate structural plasticity; they represent neither measurements nor a universal response.
